# A defect-12 \(D_{12}^{+}\) model modulo 16 at order 2396

At \(n=2396=4\cdot599\) there is an exact zero-sum shadow frame \(V\) of \(D_{12}^{+}\), with \(V^{\mathsf T}V=599I_{12}\) and row lattice \(D_{12}^{*}\), together with a normalized sign matrix \(A\) of size \(2396\times2384\). For any integral basis \(B\) of \(U=D_{12}^{+}\) in columns, set
\[
 K=B^{\mathsf T}B,\qquad C=2VB,\qquad P=[A\ C],\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(I_{2384},K).
\]
The matrices \(P\) and \(D^{-1}\) are integral, and
\[
 P^{\mathsf T}P\equiv2396D\pmod{16},\qquad
 PD^{-1}P^{\mathsf T}\equiv2396I_{2396}\pmod{16}.
\]
The row products in \(A\) distinguish the integer and half-integer shadow rows. This model uses a different frame from [MOD8_DEFECT12.md](MOD8_DEFECT12.md). The signed columns with indices 0 and 2 have inner product 32 over the integers. The exact existence question at order 2396 remains open.

## Construction and certificate

Use the 32-modular Goethals–Seidel matrix \(H\) from Shalom Eliahou and Michel Kervaire, *A survey on modular Hadamard matrices*, **Discrete Mathematics** 302 (2005), 85–106, array (3) and Theorem 3.2.2. The authors give four length-\(t\) sequences for \(t=4r+3\), and the array has both Gram matrices congruent to \(4tI\) modulo 32. The [open-access survey version](https://morfismos.cinvestav.mx/sites/default/files/Upload/vol07-n2-2.pdf) contains the sequences and array.

For \(t=599\), put \(r=149\) and use
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F &= [1^{148};-1,1,1;(-1,1)^{74}],\\
 G &= [(-1)^{148};1,-1,1;(-1,1)^{74}],\\
 (a,b,c,d)&=([F;1;-F^\#],[F;1;-G^\#],[G;1;-F^\#],[G;-1;-G^\#]).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The exponent on an entry or list denotes repetition. For a sequence \(f\), \(f^\#\) reverses the entries \((-1)^i f_i\). Our circulants use entry \(f_{j-i}\), and the reversal matrix sends \(j\) to \(-j\) modulo 599.

With zero-based indices, take the four rows and twelve columns
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I&=(898,899,2096,2097),\\
 J&=(0,1,150,300,898,1048,1198,1497,1647,1796,2096,2246).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The rows in \(I\) are pairwise exactly orthogonal in \(H\). The columns in \(J\cup\{149\}\) also are pairwise exactly orthogonal. Normalize column 149 by row signs. The tetrad product is now negative exactly on \(J\).

Apply the reflection \(T=J_4/2-I_4\) on those four rows. The tetrad entries outside \(J\) stay signs, and its entries in \(J\) become one \(2\) or \(-2\) and three zeros in each column. Keep the sign columns as \(A\), moving column 149 and row 898 to the first positions and normalizing the first row by column signs. The other columns after reflection give \(R=2V\), with its first column negated to use the shadow convention of [MOD8_DEFECT12.md](MOD8_DEFECT12.md).

The twelve columns in \(R\) remain exactly orthogonal and of squared norm 2396. Their sums vanish because the first column of \(A\) is all ones and was exactly orthogonal to the columns in \(J\). The tetrad rows have squared norm 12 in \(R\) because the four source rows were exactly orthogonal and the 2384 entries of \(A\) are signs. The twelve nonzero tetrad entries occupy disjoint triples. The other rows of \(R\) are sign rows. The doubled rows all have coordinate sum 2 modulo 4.

Before the final row permutation, the minor of \(R\) on rows
\[
 (0,1,4,3,301,151,149,154,302,150,450,451)
\]
has determinant \(16384=2^{14}\). Thus the corresponding half rows generate a sublattice of \(D_{12}^{*}\) of covolume 4 and index 8. Let \(L\) be the lattice generated by all rows of \(V\). Both shadow types are present, so \(L+D_{12}=D_{12}^{*}\). The frame identity gives \(599D_{12}\subseteq L\) as in [DEFECT12_EXACT.md](DEFECT12_EXACT.md). The index \([D_{12}^{*}:L]\) divides 8 and is odd, so \(L=D_{12}^{*}\). The even-total row combinations are precisely \(U\).

The column Gram congruence of \([A\ R]\) holds modulo 32 because the reflection is orthogonal. Replacing \(R\) by \(C=RB\) gives \(C^{\mathsf T}A\equiv0\bmod16\), since \(2B\) is integral. Also \(C^{\mathsf T}C=2396K\) exactly.

For the row Gram, the original error is \(32E=HH^{\mathsf T}-2396I\) with \(E\) integral, symmetric and zero on the diagonal. After normalizing by row signs and applying the reflection, terms between the tetrad and outside rows remain multiples of 16. Inside the tetrad, writing \(E_I\) for the signed error block, the error becomes \(8(J_4-2I_4)E_I(J_4-2I_4)\). The entries of the product without the factor 8 are even, since each is congruent to the sum of all entries of \(E_I\), which is even by symmetry and the zero diagonal. Thus
\[
 AA^{\mathsf T}+RR^{\mathsf T}
 = PD^{-1}P^{\mathsf T}\equiv2396I\pmod{16}.
\]
The [standalone verifier](defect12_mod16.py) reconstructs these matrices using the Python standard library. It checks the exact frame identities and minor, both Gram congruences, and the row product condition directly. Its integral \(U\) basis is the usual \(D_{12}\) root basis with \(e_0-e_1\) replaced by \(\tfrac12\mathbf1\). This replacement halves the covolume from 2 to 1.

The same tetrad pattern, with a parity-dependent affine minor certificate, gives a model for every multiplier \(t=4r+3\), \(r\ge1\). At orders 28, 44, and 60 it gives exact maximal partial Hadamards. [Parametric construction and certificates](MOD16_DEFECT12_FAMILY.md)

## A general reduction for the row checks

Let \(n=4t\) with \(t\) odd. Let \(P\) be an integral \(n\times n\) matrix with first column \(\mathbf1_n\), and with entries in each column of constant parity. Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,D_1)\), where \(D_1\) is integral symmetric unimodular. Write \(r=P_{0,1:}\) for the row after its first entry, and assume \(rD_1^{-1}r^{\mathsf T}=n-1\). Put \(v=n-1\), \(\mathbf1=\mathbf1_v\), and
\[
 S=(P_{1:,1:}-\mathbf1r)/2,\qquad p=D_1^{-1}r^{\mathsf T}.
\]
If \(P^{\mathsf T}P\equiv nD\bmod16\), then
\[
 PD^{-1}P^{\mathsf T}\equiv nI\pmod{16}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Sp\equiv-2t\mathbf1\pmod8.
\]
Equivalently, the row Gram congruence only needs to be checked against the first row once the column Gram congruence holds.

To prove this, factor \(P\) as
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\mathbf1&I\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2S\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&r\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The column Gram assumption gives
\[
 \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}S\equiv-2tr\pmod8,\qquad
 S^{\mathsf T}S\equiv t(D_1+r^{\mathsf T}r)\pmod4 .
\]
Work over \(\mathbb Z/4\). The vector \(p\) has \(D_1\)-norm \(v\), and \(\mathbf1\) has ordinary norm \(v\), both units. The orthogonal complements thus split off. The restriction \(S:p^\perp\to\mathbf1^\perp\) is a similitude of multiplier \(t\) and is invertible, as seen by reduction modulo 2. The second congruence makes \(Sp\) orthogonal to \(\mathbf1^\perp\), and the first shows \(Sp\equiv-2t\mathbf1\bmod4\). It follows that \(SD_1^{-1}S^{\mathsf T}\) is \(t\) times the identity on \(\mathbf1^\perp\) and zero along \(\mathbf1\). Hence \(SD_1^{-1}S^{\mathsf T}\equiv t(I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T})\bmod4\), since \(v\equiv-1\bmod4\).

Using the factorization in the row Gram leaves only the off-diagonal block \(2Sp\) to check modulo 16. More explicitly, its error in the original coordinates is
\[
 PD^{-1}P^{\mathsf T}-nI\equiv
 8(\mathbf1_n\tilde d^{\mathsf T}+\tilde d\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T})\pmod{16},
 \qquad \tilde d=\left(0,\frac{Sp+2t\mathbf1}{4}\bmod2\right).
\]
The sum of the entries of \(\tilde d\) is even by the column margin relation. This reduces the extra row conditions to a binary vector of length \(n-1\).
