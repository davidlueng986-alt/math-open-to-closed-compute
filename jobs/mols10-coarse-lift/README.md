# E073: Binary lifting of four affine OA(25,5,5,2) layers

Original problem: OA(100,5,10,2), equivalently 3-MOLS(10), remains OPEN.

## Exact restricted family and local computation

Let C={(x,y,x+y,x+2y,x+3y): x,y in F5}. Consider coarse multisets H formed by four cosets of C in F5^5, allowing repeated cosets. Is there an assignment of one fine bit to each coordinate of every coarse row such that the 100 fine rows are an OA(100,5,10,2)?

Candidate restricted nonexistence result: no. The local C++ census completed with 21,824 representatives, 31 eliminated by the two-pairs lemma, 570 by the duplicate-four-agreement lemma, 18,006 by the rank lemma and 3,217 by complete CSP search. 0 unknown; 1,278,757 CSP nodes. This was reproduced locally by two implementations of the fiber indexing (one exploiting common translations, one using explicit 100-row stacks). No independent third-party validation or novelty claim.

## Reductions and proofs

1. Coset representatives are offsets (0,0,u,v,w). Translate the four offsets by any selected offset, multiply all coordinates by any nonzero F5 scalar, then sort the four offsets. These are genuine coarse symbol relabelings and layer reorderings, preserving lift existence. Enumerate all multisets normalized to contain offset 0 and choose the lexicographic minimum among those equivalences. If three layers coincide, three rows have the same five coarse coordinates; their fine bit strings would have pairwise Hamming distance at least 4, impossible because the total distance of three binary strings of length 5 is at most 10.

2. For any coordinate i and coarse symbol u, there are 20 bit variables indexed by layer (4) and the value of another coordinate (5). For each other direction j and coarse value v, the corresponding bucket contains one row per layer. Its four bits in direction i must contain exactly two ones. The GF(2) parity equations have a 3-dimensional subspace of layer-constant even-parity assignments. If rank=17, this is the whole kernel. Each balanced assignment in such a rigid fiber partitions the four layers into two pairs, up to bit complement, in one of three ways, and that partition is constant across all buckets. If at least four distinct directions have a rigid fiber, choose one rigid fiber per direction: any pair must use different partitions, impossible with only three partitions.

3. General exact CSP: enumerate the full binary nullspace for each of 25 fibers, retaining assignments with exactly two ones in all 20 buckets. Fix its first bit to zero (per-fiber symbol swap) and deduplicate by the 20 partition colors. For two fibers in distinct directions, the bucket common to them yields all four fine pairs exactly when their partitions differ. Thus pairwise color inequality is necessary and sufficient. Arc consistency removes a color when the opposite fiber has only that color available at the shared bucket. Branch over every remaining domain value; a node cap yields UNKNOWN, never UNSAT. A SAT witness is rebuilt into 100 five-coordinate rows and all ten pair projections checked.

4. Duplicate-four-agreement lemma: distinct fine rows share at most one fine coordinate. If two coarse rows coincide, their five fine bits differ in at least four positions. If another coarse row shares at least four coarse positions with them, at least three of those positions have opposite fine bits in the duplicates. The other row must agree with one of the duplicates in each such position, forcing at least two agreements with one of them. Contradiction.

5. Two-pairs lemma: suppose the layers are C,C,D,D with distinct cosets C and D. In each coarse fiber (i,u), write d_C(t) for the XOR of the bits on the duplicated C row and d_D(t) for the analogous XOR in D. Parameter t is the value in a different direction. Pair-bucket parity forces d_C(t)=d_D(t+s_j) for each other direction j, since equal-slope affine fibers correspond by a translation s_j of F5. Not all four translations can be equal: otherwise a C row and D row agree in all five coarse coordinates, impossible for distinct cosets. Two different shifts force d_C and d_D constant in t because a nonzero shift generates F5. Hence the duplicate XOR is determined solely by (i,u), and has the same value for both cosets. At most one direction has any zero fiber: if two directions have zero fibers, their pair of coarse values occurs in C and two duplicate rows agree in two fine coordinates. Thus at least four directions are complementary everywhere. For each pair of these directions i,j the two fine pairs from C are complementary, and those from D must be the other complementary pair. Let f_i and g_i be the bits on one copy of C and D. Summing the XOR relation f_i+f_j+g_i+g_j=1 over the 25 coarse (i,j) pairs, each C and D row once, gives s_i+s_j=1 in F2, where s_i is the sum of f_i and g_i over their respective 25 rows. Three complementary directions already give a contradiction by summing the three equations.

All formulas above apply only to the specified four-coset construction except the generic fiber CSP, rank lemma and duplicate-distance lemma, which apply to stacks of any four OA(25,5,5,2) layers.

## Direct constructive extension

The same exact fiber solver is used in a rank-guided mutation search over four independently isotoped affine OA5 layers, using coordinate and symbol permutations and avoiding the exhausted aligned coset family. A 6s local pilot tested 130,946 mutation proposals, with 53,828 distinct hash-filtered viable stacks, 53,826 CSP-refuted, 2 capped UNKNOWN, no witness. These heuristic counts do not cover the enlarged family; hash deduplication is used only for the heuristic. The workflow replays the exact census and spends 120s per shard looking for a witness beyond it, preserving unknown stacks for followup. A witness anywhere would resolve the original YES question. Failure of this restricted construction does not resolve the original NO question.
